Philosophy
The Level 5 Mindset
  • Machine learning is NOT black-box guesswork or blind copy-pasting.
  • It is NOT just dry proofs either.
  • It is empirical software engineering:
  • 1. Formulate a real-world problem mathematically.
  • 2. Build and train models that learn from empirical data.
  • 3. Critically evaluate performance, biases, and edge cases.

Pedagogy Core Learning Pillars

How We Are Going to Learn: 3 Pillars

Pillar 1
Grounded Intuition
  • Understand why an algorithm works before touching libraries.
  • Visualize loss landscapes and optimization geometries.
  • No memorizing formulas without geometric meaning.
Pillar 2
Production Craft
  • Implement algorithms from scratch in NumPy to understand the mechanics.
  • Then use production-standard Scikit-Learn pipelines.
  • Containerized, reproducible experiments with Docker.
Pillar 3
Critical Evaluation
  • High training accuracy is often an illusion.
  • Audit models for overfitting, leakage, and data bias.
  • Evaluate honestly with held-out validation and test sets.
Standards UWE Bristol Level 5

University Expectations & Academic Rigor

Level 5 Expectations
Intellectual Ownership
  • You are the author and architect of your own models and experimental setups.
  • Passive listening in class will not prepare you for module submissions.
  • Read primary documentation, experiment proactively, and ask challenging questions.
  • Attendance and active lab participation are mandatory.
Empirical Rigor
The Scientific Standard
  • Never state "my model works" without showing precision, recall, confusion matrices, and baselines.
  • Every claim must be backed by reproducible experiments.
  • Written reports must justify hyperparameter choices and discuss limitations.
  • Academic integrity: All sources and AI tooling must be clearly referenced.
Accountability Course Rules

The Non-Negotiable "Must-Do's"

Rule 1 & 2
Environment & Cell Execution
  • 1. Week 0 Setup Ready Before Class 2: Docker and GitHub verified. No debugging installations during lecture hours.
  • 2. All Notebook Cells Must Be Executed: Submitting a notebook with unexecuted (empty) cells is an immediate failing grade. Cell outputs are proof of execution.
Rule 3 & 4
Commit Cadence & Authorship
  • 3. Incremental Git Commits: Commit your progress regularly. Submitting an entire project in a single commit 5 minutes before deadline is an academic red flag.
  • 4. Individual Authorship (Group Work: No): This module is 100% individual. Personal commit history in the course organization verifies your authentic work.
